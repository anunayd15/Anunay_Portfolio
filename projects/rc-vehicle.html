Custom-Built RC Vehicle

OVERVIEW

Designing a fully functional RC vehicle from individual mechanical and electrical subsystems

The goal was to design, fabricate, and integrate a functional remote-controlled vehicle while balancing vehicle size, weight, steering performance, drivetrain efficiency, and manufacturability.

The vehicle was developed around a four-wheel layout with a 9-inch wheelbase and 6-inch track width. The final design incorporated a belt-driven drivetrain, servo-actuated Ackermann steering, a custom chassis, and onboard electronics into a compact platform.

VEHICLE ARCHITECTURE

What were the four primary systems?

Chassis

The chassis was developed around a lightweight laser cut wood structure that could support the drivetrain, steering system, electronics, and wheel assemblies. The chassis geometry was laid out around the vehicle's 9-inch wheelbase and 6-inch track width, then fabricated and assembled everything together using screws.

Belt Drivetrain

The drivetrain used a belt transmission to transfer power from the rear-mounted motor to the driven axle. A final gear ratio of approximately 2.83:1 was calculated to meet the vehicle's target acceleration while maintaining sufficient vehicle speed. During fabrication, the rear axle was connected to the belt transmission using epoxy because the purchased components did not provide a direct mechanical fastening interface. The connection functioned during testing, but later became a structural weak point after an impact, highlighting the need for a stronger fastened axle connection.

Ackermann Steering

The steering system was designed around Ackermann geometry so the inner and outer front wheels could follow different turning radii during cornering. A servo actuated a multi-bar linkage that transferred motion to the front wheels. The custom steering components and wheel interfaces were developed through SolidWorks and manufactured using 3D printing, allowing the linkage geometry and component fit to be adjusted during assembly.

Electronics & Control

The electrical system combined a brushed drive motor, steering servo, electronic speed controller, 7.2 V battery, and wireless transmitter/receiver to provide throttle and steering control. The components were arranged within the limited chassis space while maintaining access to the mechanical systems for assembly and troubleshooting. This allowed the drivetrain and steering subsystems to be controlled together as a complete RC vehicle.

OUTCOME

Integrating the individual systems into a functional vehicle

The completed RC vehicle successfully brought the chassis, drivetrain, steering system, electronics, and controls together into a single functional platform.

Testing also exposed several areas where the physical vehicle behaved differently than expected from the initial design. One of the primary issues occurred at the axle-transmission connection, which became a structural weak point after impact.

Epoxy was used to reinforce the connection and provide additional structural support, allowing the drivetrain to remain integrated with the vehicle. Through these adjustments and final system integration, the vehicle was able to successfully drive, steer, and demonstrate the intended overall functionality.

The project provided hands-on experience taking a vehicle from initial mechanical layout through fabrication, integration, troubleshooting, and physical performance testing.
